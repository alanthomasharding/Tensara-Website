Women's health · Soft tissue repair

Next-generation soft tissue repair in women's health and beyond.

Tensara is developing a resorbable nanofiber scaffold that guides the body to rebuild its own organized tissue, then dissolves, leaving no permanent mesh behind.

The problem

Existing mesh struggles to meet the standard of care.

Surgeons repairing soft tissue today choose between permanent plastic, current resorbables that don't guide healing, or a second surgery to harvest the patient's own tissue.

Permanent polypropylene mesh

  • Permanent foreign material
  • Associated with chronic inflammation and erosion
  • Forms scar tissue rather than regenerating tissue
  • Under increased regulatory scrutiny

Current bioresorbable mesh

  • Macroporous and abrasive structure
  • Limited or no control over cell recruitment
  • Little control over how tissue regenerates

Autologous fascia

  • Requires a second surgical site
  • Donor-site morbidity
  • Longer recovery
300,000 suburethral slings placed each year for stress urinary incontinence
Up to 20% clinical failure rate with current sling procedures
$15.5B U.S. soft tissue repair market

Our technology

Scaffold-directed tissue architecture and function.

Tensara's engineered nanofibers are designed to direct and organize tissue regeneration so the repaired tissue resembles the pre-trauma tissue: restoring native tissue, not replacing it.

  1. 1

    Electrospun nanofibers

    Coaxial nanofibers, around 500 nm across, mimic the body's natural extracellular matrix.

  2. 2

    Controlled tissue architecture

    An elongated, aligned fiber structure guides cells to organize the way native tissue does.

  3. 3

    Resorbable & regenerative

    The scaffold guides tissue ingrowth, then resorbs, leaving no permanent foreign body behind.
